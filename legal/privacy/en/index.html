This Privacy Policy (the "Policy") describes how Izure (the "App") handles information about its users (the "User"). The App is developed and operated by Sota Sugiyama (handle: mon50) as an individual ("we" / "us").

The App stores each short record the User places (called a "ticket" in the App) on the User's own device so that it can be opened again later. To assign titles, to show short pointers before you read a link, and to pick related tickets, the App sends the information it needs to our server and to an AI service. This Policy describes, feature by feature and based on the implementation, what is sent and where.

The Japanese version of this Policy is the authoritative original. In the event of any conflict between the Japanese and English versions, the Japanese version prevails.

1. Information We Collect

We handle the following information to the extent necessary for the App to function.

1.1 Information Stored On Your Device

The following is stored in a SQLite database on your device. It is not stored on our servers.

  • The text you enter, or that is shared to the App from another app, exactly as submitted.
  • URLs extracted from that text, and their host names; together with the opening lines of the linked page (up to 60 characters), the number of photos and videos attached, whether the page's text can be retrieved, and a flag marking links whose content we cannot see.
  • The title, summary and category the App assigns to that text.
  • Notes you add to a ticket later (called a "checkpoint" in the App).
  • What the App generates: "seeds" for tickets you placed in your own words; the short pointers shown when you open a link (what it is about, things to look for, and questions for after reading); and the questions the App asks you about links whose content it cannot see.
  • The grouping relationships between tickets (called a "stack" in the App), and the one line showing how each ticket connects to the base of the stack (paid plan "Izure Pro" (the "Plan") only).
  • Links between a newly placed ticket and earlier tickets picked as being about the same thing, with one line describing the connection.
  • The reminder time you set on a ticket, the time you settled it, and its deletion state.
  • The times you opened the original page of a link ticket (used to show "what you did this month" in the monthly review; it does not include the page's content).
  • Monthly review results (the names and sizes of the month's topics, the numbers of the words picked, the question, and the one line drawn from that month's words), your answers to "Ask yourself", and a mark for each word you tapped "Let it go" or "Make it a to-do" on in the review (a short one-way mark, not the words themselves).

This database is not encrypted by the App itself. Its protection relies on the disk encryption provided by your device's operating system. See §6 for details.

The following is stored in the operating system's secure storage (iOS Keychain / Android Keystore):

  • Theme mode, accent color, notification settings, and the onboarding-completed flag.
  • Display state: your choice for the "Try it" list (which introduces features one at a time) and the items you have completed, the month in which you tapped the monthly-review prompt, a record of when a Plan prompt was shown, and similar.
  • The anonymous device identifier (§1.3).
  • A cached copy of your purchase status (active or not, expiry date, purchased product identifier).
  • The access token the App uses to reach our server.

1.2 Information Sent To Our Server, Feature by Feature

For the following operations, the App transmits information over the Internet to our stateless server (running on Cloudflare Workers). Our server passes what it receives (and any page content retrieved under §3.2) to the OpenAI API (a large language model), obtains the generated result, and returns it to your device. Our server does not store what it receives. OpenAI's handling is described in §4.2.

Operations available on both the free tier and the Plan

  • Placing a ticket (including re-reading a ticket that was placed without reaching the AI): the text you entered or shared, exactly as submitted; URLs extracted from that text (up to four); and your device's local time (including its time-zone offset). If there are URLs, our server retrieves the title, description and opening text from them (§3.2) as material for the title, summary and category.
  • Picking earlier tickets about the same thing (done automatically right after you place a ticket): the new ticket's title, its text without URLs (up to 300 characters), the page's opening lines and host name, and the titles and identifiers of earlier tickets (newest first, up to thirty). For tickets whose link content we cannot see, the last checkpoint you wrote is added to the title.
  • Opening a link ticket to prepare short pointers before reading: the ticket's URL, title, the note you wrote when placing it, and the page's opening lines. Our server retrieves the page (§3.2) and passes up to the first 4,000 characters of its text and its title, together with your note, to the OpenAI API. If no body text is available and only a title and description can be retrieved, only the title and the page's opening lines are passed. If the generated pointers repeat 20 or more consecutive characters of the retrieved text, the App has them rewritten or does not show that part. The pointers are stored on your device and are not generated again for the same ticket.
  • Choosing questions for links whose content we cannot see: for Instagram, Facebook, Threads and TikTok links, and for pages that require a login, that decline retrieval (§3.3), or that could not be retrieved, no page content is used. Instead, the host name, words that can be read from the URL with its query and "#" fragment removed, the ticket's title (your own words, or just the service name) and the note you wrote when placing it are passed to the OpenAI API to choose questions to ask you.
  • Re-titling a link whose content we cannot see, from your answer: the current title, the host name, and the answer you wrote (a checkpoint).
  • Generating "seeds" for a ticket placed in your own words: the text as placed (up to 2,000 characters), the date and body of the checkpoints you wrote (up to twenty), and the titles of other tickets placed around the same time (within seven days before or after, up to eight).

Plan operations

  • Ask to find: the question you type, and for each of your tickets (up to 150) its title, the note written when placing it, the page's opening lines, host name, your checkpoints, how many days ago it was placed, whether it is settled, and its identifier.
  • Monthly review (Plan): the words you wrote when placing tickets that month (text without URLs) and your checkpoints; the titles of the links you placed that month and the "what it is about" line of their pointers (if already made); the date of each; and the names and sizes of last month's topics, if the review for last month is kept on your device. Up to 200 entries, each up to 160 characters, are passed to the AI. To-dos and samples are not sent. The times you opened original pages and your answers to "Ask yourself" are not sent.
  • One line connecting a stack: the title of the stack's base and the note written when placing it, and the titles and identifiers of the tickets stacked on it (up to twelve).
  • Finding related articles from outside: the title of the stack's base and its note, and the titles and URLs of the stacked tickets (up to twelve). Our server passes the titles and note to OpenAI's web search to look for candidates (the URLs are used only to remove duplicates and are not passed to the search). Our server retrieves each candidate page once to confirm that it opens (§3.2), then passes the candidates' titles and host names, together with the base's title and note and the stacked tickets' titles, to the OpenAI API to pick up to three. This operation has a monthly limit.

What we do not send to our server: the App does not send your conversation history with the AI, or images. "Export" (Plan) builds its text on your device and only hands it to the operating system's share feature; nothing is sent to our server. Choosing a notification time is also handled only on your device.

1.3 Identifiers Accompanying Those Requests

The requests described in §1.2 carry the following:

  • An anonymous device identifier: a random UUID generated by the App on first launch. It is not linked to your name, email address, store account, or any advertising identifier. We use it to throttle bursts of requests and to count monthly use of the feature that has a monthly limit (finding from outside). Only an 8-character hash of this value is retained on our server side; the original value cannot be recovered from it.
  • Your current plan type (free or paid), used to decide which limits apply and whether Plan features are available.
  • Your device's display language (Japanese or English), used to choose the language the AI writes in and the language preference sent to sites when retrieving pages.
  • A per-request ID: a random value the App creates for each request. It is used only to match our server's logs with the diagnostic information in §1.7. It is separate from the device identifier, changes with every request, and is not linked to you.
  • An access token shared by all installations of the App. It lets our server confirm that a request comes from the App. It is not unique per User and contains no information that identifies you.

1.4 Device Permissions

  • Notifications: when you set a time on a ticket, the App requests notification permission so that it can notify you at that time. All notifications are local notifications scheduled on your device; they are not push notifications sent from our server. A notification's body contains the title of the ticket. You can turn notifications off at any time in the App's settings.
  • Photo library, camera, location, contacts, microphone, call logs: not declared and not collected. The App does not handle images.

1.5 Receiving Content From Other Apps (Share Sheet)

You can send text or a URL to the App from another app's share menu (iOS and Android). When you do, the App's own screen does not open; the shared string is held temporarily in a file on your device (on iOS, in an on-device area shared by the App and its share-menu receiver). The next time you open or return to the App, that file is read, the content is saved as a ticket in the on-device database, and it is transmitted to our server as a "placing a ticket" operation under §1.2. Once read, the holding file is deleted.

Where the sharing app supplies a subject line (Android's EXTRA_SUBJECT) that duplicates the body, the App removes it. Only text and URLs are accepted; images are not.

If you share a third party's post or page, its content travels to our server and to OpenAI through the flow described in §1.2 and §3.2. For the treatment of third-party rights, please see the Terms of Service.

1.6 Purchase and Subscription Information

If you purchase the Plan, the payment itself is processed by the Apple App Store / Google Play. To manage your purchase status (whether the subscription is active, and its expiry date), we send the purchase receipt, the store-issued purchase identifier, and an anonymous in-app user ID generated by RevenueCat (not linked to your name or other identifying information) to our processor RevenueCat (see §4.1). Neither we nor RevenueCat collect or retain your credit card number or other payment details.

When you use a Plan feature, the App also sends this anonymous in-app user ID to our server, and our server asks RevenueCat whether the subscription is active. Our server keeps the ID and the answer (active or not) in memory for up to 10 minutes only, and does not store them.

The App has no sign-in; your purchase is tied to your store account. We do not offer a free trial.

1.7 Diagnostic Information When Something Fails

When an operation in §1.2 fails (our server could not be reached, the response took too long, the response was cut off partway, an error was returned, and so on), the App creates diagnostic information so that we can find the cause, and sends it to our server. Because a failure often means the server cannot be reached, the App first keeps this information on your device (up to 50 entries; beyond that, the oldest are discarded) and sends it the next time a request to our server succeeds, or when the App starts. Entries are removed from your device once sent.

Diagnostic information contains only the following:

  • Which kind of operation failed (placing a ticket, reading it again, pointers before reading, ask to find, the connecting line of a stack, finding from outside, the monthly review, seeds, and so on)
  • The kind of reason, from a fixed set (busy, limit reached, Plan required, could not connect, timed out, malformed response, cut off partway, etc.)
  • Where it stopped (connecting / waiting for the response / while receiving) and how long it took
  • The App version, the operating system and its version, and your plan type (free or paid)
  • The domain name of the site involved (for example example.com). The URL's path, query and anything after "#" are not included
  • The per-request ID (§1.3)

Not included: the text, title or note of any ticket, checkpoints, questions or any words you wrote, any part of a URL other than the domain name, the anonymous device identifier, and the RevenueCat in-app user ID. Before sending, the App checks again that only the fixed items are present; our server performs the same check on receipt and discards anything else. Diagnostic information is not linked to you and is not used for tracking (following you across other companies' apps or websites).

1.8 Information We Do Not Collect

  • Account information (the App has no sign-up): name, address, phone number, date of birth, gender, email address.
  • Advertising identifiers (IDFA / Advertising ID).
  • Third-party crash reporting or analytics SDKs (Firebase / Crashlytics / Google Analytics / Sentry, etc.). The App embeds none of these. Diagnostic information about failures is sent only to our own server, as described in §1.7.
  • Advertising SDKs. The App contains no advertising.

  • The information in §1.6 is used solely to manage purchase status and entitlements.
  • The diagnostic information in §1.7 and the per-request ID in §1.3 are used solely to find and fix the causes of failures, and to notice when failures increase.

Legal basis (GDPR): processing under §1.2, §1.3 and §1.6 is necessary for the performance of the service requested by the User (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)). The diagnostic information in §1.7 is processed on the basis of our legitimate interest in keeping the service working (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)), and is limited to items that are not linked to you.

3.2 Pages Our Server Retrieves

In the following cases, our server accesses the destination site directly and retrieves the page's title, description and body text.

  • When you place a ticket: URLs contained in the text (up to four). About the first 1,200 characters of text are used and passed to the OpenAI API as material for the title.
  • When you first open a link ticket: that ticket's URL. Up to the first 4,000 characters of its text are passed to the OpenAI API as material for the pointers before reading (§1.2).
  • When you look for related articles from outside (Plan): candidate pages found by web search (up to eight). They are retrieved only to confirm that they open and to read their titles; their text is not passed to the OpenAI API. Only the title and host name are passed.

Retrieval is handled as follows.

  • The access is made by our server, not by your device. Your IP address is not disclosed to the destination site.
  • If the destination does not respond, or the content is neither HTML nor plain text, no retrieval takes place and processing continues without it.
  • No retrieval is attempted for localhost, private IP addresses, or non-standard ports.
  • For X (formerly Twitter), we retrieve a post's text and the number of attached photos and videos from the syndication endpoint X publishes for embedding (falling back to the public oEmbed endpoint). The photos and videos themselves are not retrieved. For YouTube, we retrieve the title and channel name from the public oEmbed endpoint and the description from the video's page. The video itself is not retrieved.
  • For Instagram, Facebook, Threads and TikTok links, we retrieve neither the page nor any embedding endpoint. Such tickets are labelled by service (for example "Instagram post") and by the words you write.
  • Retrieved page content is not stored on our server.

3.3 What Our Server Identifies Itself As When Fetching, and the Scope of Fetching

When making the fetches described in §3.2, our server sends the following name (the HTTP User-Agent header) to the destination site. It never identifies itself as another service's crawler or as a browser.

IzureBot/1.0 (+https://mon50.github.io/izure-legal/)

The URL in parentheses is this App's legal information page. It is included so that the administrator of the destination site can see where the request came from and contact us.

We fetch only within the following limits.

  • We fetch only in response to your own action. We fetch only URLs you have placed, and candidate pages found when you press "find from outside."
  • We do not fetch the same URL again and again. A URL you place is fetched once when you place it and once when you first open that ticket. The outcome is kept on your device. Only if a fetch failed temporarily is it tried again the next time you open the ticket. Candidate pages found from outside are fetched once per search.
  • We do not crawl. We never follow links contained in a page we have fetched.
  • We do not fetch destinations on localhost, private IP addresses, or non-standard ports (§3.2).

Before fetching a page, we check the destination site's robots.txt and follow any Disallow rules addressed to IzureBot or to *. If a fetched page declares noai, noindex, noarchive or none in a meta tag or an X-Robots-Tag header, we do not use that page's body text or description. In either case the App continues with only the title and words you placed (and a candidate found from outside is not shown). The embedding endpoints that X and YouTube publish (such as oEmbed) are not web pages and are not subject to this check. YouTube video pages are subject to it.

If you administer a destination site and would prefer that we not fetch from it, please tell us at the contact point in §9 and we will stop fetching from that site.

Where the content of a page cannot be retrieved — because a login is required, because the page has been removed, or for any other reason — we continue without that content. For a link ticket, the App shows questions for you instead of pointers before reading (§1.2).

4.2 How OpenAI Handles This Data

According to OpenAI's published documentation on API data controls, content sent through the API is not used to train or improve OpenAI's models unless the developer explicitly opts in. We have not opted in.

However, OpenAI may retain records of API activity for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring. After that period, such records are removed from their systems unless longer retention is required by law. The fact that our server does not store content and the fact that OpenAI may hold records for a limited period are therefore two separate matters.

We are not currently covered by Zero Data Retention (ZDR), which would exclude our traffic from those abuse-monitoring records.

Web search when finding from outside uses the web search feature of the OpenAI API. The search request (the stack base's title and note and the stacked tickets' titles) is used by OpenAI to carry out the search.

4.3 International Transfers

The processors above are located in the United States. In addition, Cloudflare Workers runs at edge locations worldwide, so the country in which processing occurs varies from request to request.

Transfers from the European Economic Area (EEA) and the United Kingdom to the United States rely on the Standard Contractual Clauses (GDPR Art. 46) or equivalent mechanisms maintained by each processor. For details of the safeguards each applies, please see their respective privacy policies.

5. Storage Locations and Retention

5.1 On-Device Data

  • Ticket text, titles, summaries, checkpoints, pointers before reading, questions, seeds, stack and link relationships, the times you opened original pages, and monthly review results and answers are stored only on your device.
  • We do not unilaterally set a retention period. Tickets do not become unavailable because of how long ago they were placed.
  • Uninstalling the App removes the on-device database and the share-holding file. Settings and identifiers held in the operating system's secure storage are removed in accordance with the operating system's behaviour.

5.2 Server Side

  • Our server does not store the text of your tickets, their titles, your notes, checkpoints or questions, the content retrieved from URLs, or web search results.
  • To enforce the monthly limit on finding from outside (Plan), we keep a per-calendar-month (Japan Standard Time) request counter in a Cloudflare Durable Object, keyed by the hashed device identifier. This counter contains no content.
  • To throttle bursts of requests, we keep per-hashed-identifier counters over one-minute and one-day windows in the same way.
  • For operational monitoring and cost management, we log the request path, HTTP status, error type, hashed identifier, timestamp, and numbers such as counts, durations and token usage to Cloudflare's logs. Ticket text, titles, notes, checkpoints, URLs and search requests are not logged. When the App attaches a per-request ID (§1.3), that ID is logged as well.
  • The diagnostic information in §1.7 is stored in Cloudflare Workers Analytics Engine. It is kept for three months and then deleted automatically. We count failures over the last 15 minutes by kind of operation and kind of reason, and when there are many, send only those counts to our own Slack.

5.3 What "Delete everything" Actually Does

When you run "Delete everything" in the App's settings, the following are deleted as rows from the on-device database. They are not merely marked as deleted and excluded from further use.

  • The ticket text, and the titles, summaries and search text the App assigned
  • Theme labels, URL references (including page opening lines and similar), and references to attached images
  • Checkpoints, pointers before reading, questions, "seeds," and stack and link relationships
  • The times you opened original pages, monthly review results, your answers to "Ask yourself", and the marks for words you tapped in the review

Deleting rows alone can leave the text inside unused space in the database file. The App therefore overwrites with zeros the space a row occupied at the moment that row is deleted.

In addition, after a deletion the App rewrites the database file itself so that the unused space is reclaimed. This rewrite can fail — for example when there is not enough free space on the device. If it fails, the App tries again at the next deletion. Even when the rewrite does not happen, the space the deleted rows occupied has already been overwritten with zeros, as described above.

The following are not covered by "Delete everything."

  • The on-device record of operations (for deletions, and for changes of ticket type and completion: the kind of operation, the identifier of the target, counts and timestamps). It contains no ticket text, titles, summaries, theme labels or URLs.
  • Settings, the anonymous device identifier and the cached purchase status held in the operating system's secure storage. The anonymous device identifier is kept because it is used to manage usage limits (§5.2). However, the record that you finished the first-run guide and your progress in "Try it" are cleared, so both start from the beginning the next time you open the App.
  • Content received through the share sheet that has not yet become a ticket (§5.1). It is picked up the next time you open the App, after which that holding file is empty.
  • Diagnostic information not yet sent (§1.7; it contains no ticket text, titles or URL paths). It is sent the next time a request reaches our server, and is then removed from your device.

Running "Delete everything" also cancels every notification the App had scheduled with the operating system.

Running "Delete everything" does not cancel your subscription to the Plan, and billing continues. To cancel, use your store's subscription settings (Terms of Use §8). The same applies if you uninstall the App.

To erase all on-device data, uninstall the App. Because our server does not retain content (see §5.2), no separate deletion request to us is required.

6. Security

  • Encryption in transit: traffic between your device and our server, and between our server and OpenAI, uses HTTPS (TLS). Traffic between our server and the destination sites described in §3.2 uses HTTPS where the destination supports it.
  • On-device protection: tickets and what the App generates are stored in a local SQLite database. The App does not apply its own encryption (such as SQLCipher) to this database. Protection relies on the operating-system disk encryption (iOS File Protection / Android File-Based Encryption). We therefore strongly recommend enabling a passcode or biometric lock and keeping your OS up to date.
  • Protection of settings and identifiers: the access token, the anonymous device identifier, the cached purchase status and app settings are stored in the operating system's secure storage (iOS Keychain / Android Keystore).
  • Access control: our server requires an access token; requests without one are rejected with HTTP 401. Request bodies are also size-limited.
  • Hashed identifiers: the identifiers in our server-side counters and logs are one-way SHA-256 hashes from which the original identifier cannot be recovered.
  • Data minimization on the server: by not storing content, we reduce the risk of a server-side leak.

7. Your Rights

You have the following rights with respect to your information:

  • Access: every ticket and everything the App has generated that is stored on your device can be viewed in the App. Because we hold no copy on our servers, there is no further information for us to disclose.
  • Deletion: use "Delete everything" in the App's settings, or uninstall the App. The difference between the two is described in §5.3.
  • Correction: on-device tickets can be corrected using the App's edit features (where available), or by deleting a ticket and placing it again.
  • Data portability (GDPR Art. 20): your data resides on your own device and we hold no copy of it. With the Plan's "Export," you can hand your tickets and checkpoints to another app, as plain text or in Markdown, through the operating system's share feature. Because we retain no data server-side, there is no data for us to provide.
  • Restriction and objection: if you do not wish the transmissions described in §1.2 to occur, do not perform the operations listed there. Placing a ticket, and picking earlier tickets about the same thing right after it, happen every time you place a ticket. Unless you perform those operations, no ticket text is transmitted to our server. The diagnostic information in §1.7 is created only when an operation in §1.2 fails. Because it is not linked to you, we cannot single out a particular User's diagnostic information to disclose or delete it; it is deleted automatically three months after it is stored (§5.2).
  • Complaints: contact us using the address in §9 with any complaint regarding this Policy or our handling of your information.
  • Regulatory complaints: EEA / UK users may lodge a complaint with their local data protection authority; users in Japan may contact the Personal Information Protection Commission.

Do-not-sell declaration (CCPA): we do not sell the personal information of any User, including California residents.
