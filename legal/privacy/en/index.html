This Privacy Policy (the "Policy") describes how Izure (formerly Tsumu; the "App") handles information about its users (the "User"). The App is developed and operated by Sota Sugiyama (handle: mon50) as an individual ("we" / "us").

The App stores each short record the User places (called a "ticket" in the App) on the User's own device, and generates a piece of reading material when the User opens a ticket. This Policy describes, based on the implementation, what is sent and where.

The Japanese version of this Policy is the authoritative original. In the event of any conflict between the Japanese and English versions, the Japanese version prevails.

1. Information We Collect

We handle the following information to the extent necessary for the App to function.

1.1 Information Stored On Your Device

The following is stored in a SQLite database on your device. It is not stored on our servers.

  • The text you enter, or that is shared to the App from another app, exactly as submitted.
  • URLs extracted from that text, and their host names.
  • The title, summary and category the App assigns to that text.
  • Notes you add to a ticket later (called a "checkpoint" in the App).
  • Reading material ("catch-up") and "seeds" generated by the App, and the name of the model used to generate them.
  • The grouping relationships between tickets (called a "stack" in the App).
  • The reminder time you set on a ticket, the time you settled it, and its deletion state.
  • If chat history or reminder data created by an earlier version of the App is still present, it resides in the same database.

This database is not encrypted by the App itself. Its protection relies on the disk encryption provided by your device's operating system. See §6 for details.

The following is stored in the operating system's secure storage (iOS Keychain / Android Keystore):

  • Theme mode, accent color, notification settings, and the onboarding-completed flag.
  • The anonymous device identifier (§1.3).
  • A cached copy of your purchase status (active or not, expiry date, purchased product identifier).
  • The access token the App uses to reach our server.

1.2 Information Sent To Our Server To Generate Reading Material

For the following operations, the App transmits information over the Internet to our stateless server (running on Cloudflare Workers):

  • Placing a ticket: the text you entered or shared, exactly as submitted; URLs extracted from that text (up to two); and your device's local time (including its time-zone offset).
  • Opening a ticket to generate reading material: the URL and title of that ticket (up to three for a single ticket, up to twelve for a stack); the text as placed (up to 2,000 characters); the date and body of the checkpoints you wrote; the titles and identifiers of other tickets placed around the same time (within seven days before or after, up to eight); and the composition of the stack.
  • Generating "seeds": the text as placed, the checkpoints you wrote, and the titles of other tickets placed around the same time.

Our server forwards what it receives to the OpenAI API (a large language model), obtains the generated result, and returns it to your device. Our server does not store what it receives. OpenAI's handling is described in §4.2.

What we do not send: the App does not send your conversation history with the AI, summaries of previously saved records, or images to our server.

1.3 Identifiers Accompanying Those Requests

The requests described in §1.2 carry the following:

  • An anonymous device identifier: a random UUID generated by the App on first launch. It is not linked to your name, email address, store account, or any advertising identifier. We use it to enforce the free-plan monthly limit and to throttle bursts of requests. Only an 8-character hash of this value is retained on our server side; the original value cannot be recovered from it.
  • Your current plan type (free or paid), used to decide which limits apply.
  • An access token shared by all installations of the App. It lets our server confirm that a request comes from the App. It is not unique per User and contains no information that identifies you.

1.4 Device Permissions

  • Notifications: when you set a time on a ticket, the App requests notification permission so that it can notify you at that time. All notifications are local notifications scheduled on your device; they are not push notifications sent from our server. A notification's body contains the title of the ticket. You can turn notifications off at any time in the App's settings.
  • Photo library and camera: the App declares these permissions, and they therefore appear in the store's permission listing. However, the version as of 2026-09-22 contains no screen from which you can select or take a photo. As a result, the permission prompts are never shown, and no image is sent to our server. If a future version adds image handling, we will revise this Policy before releasing it.
  • Location, contacts, microphone, call logs: not declared and not collected.

1.5 Receiving Content From Other Apps (Share Sheet)

On Android, you can send text or a URL to the App from another app's share menu. When you do, the App's own screen does not open; the shared string is held temporarily in a file on your device. The next time you open or return to the App, that file is read, the content is saved as a ticket in the on-device database, and it is transmitted to our server as a "placing a ticket" operation under §1.2. Once read, the holding file is deleted.

Where the sharing app supplies a subject line (Android's EXTRA_SUBJECT) that duplicates the body, the App removes it. Only plain text is accepted; images are not. The iOS version does not have this feature.

If you share a third party's post or page, its content travels to our server and to OpenAI through the flow described in §1.2 and §3.2. For the treatment of third-party rights, please see the Terms of Service.

1.6 Purchase and Subscription Information

If you purchase the "Izure Pro" paid plan, the payment itself is processed by the Apple App Store / Google Play. To manage your purchase status (whether the subscription is active, and its expiry date), we send the purchase receipt, the store-issued purchase identifier, and an anonymous in-app user ID generated by RevenueCat (not linked to your name or other identifying information) to our processor RevenueCat (see §4.1). Neither we nor RevenueCat collect or retain your credit card number or other payment details.

The App has no sign-in; your purchase is tied to your store account. We do not offer a free trial.

1.7 Information We Do Not Collect

  • Account information (the App has no sign-up): name, address, phone number, date of birth, gender, email address.
  • Advertising identifiers (IDFA / Advertising ID).
  • Crash reports or analytics SDKs (Firebase / Crashlytics / Google Analytics / Sentry, etc.). The App embeds none of these.
  • Advertising SDKs. The App contains no advertising.

2. How We Use Information and Our Legal Basis

  • Information sent under §1.2 is used solely to generate the reading material, titles and summaries shown to you.
  • The identifiers in §1.3 are used solely to enforce the free-plan monthly limit, to throttle bursts of requests, and to determine your plan type.
  • The information in §1.6 is used solely to manage purchase status and entitlements.
  • We do not use any information for machine-learning training, advertising, profiling, or any other secondary purpose.

Legal basis (GDPR): processing under §1.2, §1.3 and §1.6 is necessary for the performance of the service requested by the User (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)).

3.2 Fetching the URLs You Place

When the text you place contains a URL, our server accesses that URL directly and retrieves the page's title, description and body text. What it retrieves is used as material for assigning a title and for generating reading material, and is passed to the OpenAI API for that purpose.

  • The access is made by our server, not by your device. Your IP address is not disclosed to the destination site.
  • Up to two URLs are fetched when placing a ticket, and up to three when generating reading material (up to twelve for a stack).
  • The amount of text retrieved is capped (about 1,200 characters when placing a ticket; up to 24,000 characters when generating reading material).
  • If the destination does not respond, or the content is neither HTML nor plain text, no retrieval takes place and processing continues without it.
  • No retrieval is attempted for localhost, private IP addresses, or non-standard ports.
  • For X (formerly Twitter) we use the public syndication endpoint, and for YouTube the public oEmbed endpoint.
  • Retrieved page content is not stored on our server.

3.3 What Our Server Identifies Itself As When Fetching, and Our Treatment of robots.txt

When making the fetches described in §3.2, our server sends the following name (the HTTP User-Agent header) to the destination site. It never identifies itself as another service's crawler or as a browser.

Izure/1.0 (+https://mon50.github.io/tsumu-legal/)

The URL in parentheses is this App's legal information page. It is included so that the administrator of the destination site can see where the request came from and contact us.

Whether a page may be fetched is governed by the destination site's robots.txt.

  • Before fetching a page, we retrieve /robots.txt from the destination host.
  • We follow the group addressed to Izure if one is present, and otherwise the group addressed to *. If the page in question is disallowed, we do not fetch it.
  • If robots.txt does not exist (a 404, for example), or if we are unable to retrieve it, we treat the host as imposing no restriction and proceed with the fetch.
  • So as not to request robots.txt repeatedly from the same host, what we read is held in our server's working memory for ten minutes. It contains no information about you.
  • The same treatment applies to the X syndication endpoint and the YouTube oEmbed endpoint described in §3.2.

Where the content of a page cannot be retrieved — because robots.txt disallows it, because a login is required, or for any other reason — we continue without that content. If there is not enough material to generate reading material, the App tells you so on screen.

4. Third-Party Processors and International Transfers

4.1 Processors

We engage the following third parties as processors to the extent necessary for the App to function. They act on our behalf and do not receive your information as independent recipients.

  • OpenAI, L.L.C. (United States)
    • Purpose: generating titles, summaries, reading material and "seeds."
    • Information transferred: the text, URLs, titles, checkpoints, and other tickets' titles and identifiers listed in §1.2, together with the page content retrieved under §3.2.
    • Handling is described in §4.2.
  • Cloudflare, Inc. (United States)
    • Purpose: hosting our stateless server (Cloudflare Workers).
    • We do not store the text of your tickets on Cloudflare. What we store there is limited to the counters and logs described in §5.2.
  • RevenueCat, Inc. (United States)
    • Purpose: managing paid-plan (subscription) purchase status and entitlements.
    • The information sent is limited to the purchase receipt, the store purchase identifier, and an anonymous in-app user ID; it does not include ticket content or identifying information.

We do not sell, rent, or share your information with any other third party (advertisers, data brokers, or external companies).

4.2 How OpenAI Handles This Data

According to OpenAI's published documentation on API data controls, content sent through the API is not used to train or improve OpenAI's models unless the developer explicitly opts in. We have not opted in.

However, OpenAI may retain records of API activity for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring. After that period, such records are removed from their systems unless longer retention is required by law. The fact that our server does not store content and the fact that OpenAI may hold records for a limited period are therefore two separate matters.

We are not currently covered by Zero Data Retention (ZDR), which would exclude our traffic from those abuse-monitoring records.

4.3 International Transfers

The processors above are located in the United States. In addition, Cloudflare Workers runs at edge locations worldwide, so the country in which processing occurs varies from request to request.

Transfers from the European Economic Area (EEA) and the United Kingdom to the United States rely on the Standard Contractual Clauses (GDPR Art. 46) or equivalent mechanisms maintained by each processor. For details of the safeguards each applies, please see their respective privacy policies.

5. Storage Locations and Retention

5.1 On-Device Data

  • Ticket text, titles, summaries, checkpoints, reading material and stack relationships are stored only on your device.
  • We do not unilaterally set a retention period.
  • Uninstalling the App removes the on-device database and the share-holding file. Settings and identifiers held in the operating system's secure storage are removed in accordance with the operating system's behaviour.

5.2 Server Side

  • Our server does not store the text of your tickets, their titles, your checkpoints, or the content retrieved from URLs.
  • To enforce the free-plan limit, we keep a per-calendar-month (Japan Standard Time) request counter in a Cloudflare Durable Object, keyed by the hashed device identifier. This counter contains no content.
  • To throttle bursts of requests, we keep per-hashed-identifier counters over one-minute and one-day windows in the same way.
  • For operational monitoring, we log the request path, HTTP status, error type, hashed identifier and timestamp to Cloudflare's logs. Ticket text, titles, checkpoints and URLs are not logged.

5.3 What "Delete All" Actually Does

When you run "Delete All" in the App's settings, the following are deleted as rows from the on-device database. They are not merely marked as deleted and excluded from further use.

  • The ticket text, and the titles, summaries and search text the App assigned
  • Theme labels, URL references, and references to attached images
  • Checkpoints, generated reading material, "seeds," and stack relationships
  • Chat history from earlier versions

Deleting rows alone can leave the text inside unused space in the database file. The App therefore rewrites the database file after the deletion, so that the text is gone from the file as well.

Versions released before 22 September 2026 marked ticket text, titles and summaries as deleted instead, and those rows remained inside the database file. If you update from such a version, the rows left behind are deleted the same way the first time the App starts after the update.

The following are not covered by "Delete All."

  • The on-device record of operations (for deletions, and for changes of ticket type and completion: the kind of operation, the identifier of the target, counts and timestamps). It contains no ticket text, titles, summaries, theme labels or URLs.
  • Settings, the anonymous device identifier and the cached purchase status held in the operating system's secure storage. The anonymous device identifier is kept because it is used for the free-tier monthly limit (§5.2).
  • Content received through the share sheet that has not yet become a ticket (§5.1). It is picked up the next time you open the App, after which that holding file is empty.

Running "Delete All" also cancels every notification the App had scheduled with the operating system.

To erase all on-device data, uninstall the App. Because our server does not retain content (see §5.2), no separate deletion request to us is required.

6. Security

  • Encryption in transit: traffic between your device and our server, between our server and OpenAI, and between our server and the destination sites described in §3.2, all uses HTTPS (TLS).
  • On-device protection: tickets and reading material are stored in a local SQLite database. The App does not apply its own encryption (such as SQLCipher) to this database. Protection relies on the operating-system disk encryption (iOS File Protection / Android File-Based Encryption). We therefore strongly recommend enabling a passcode or biometric lock and keeping your OS up to date.
  • Protection of settings and identifiers: the access token, the anonymous device identifier, the cached purchase status and app settings are stored in the operating system's secure storage (iOS Keychain / Android Keystore).
  • Access control: our server requires an access token; requests without one are rejected with HTTP 401. Request bodies are also size-limited.
  • Hashed identifiers: the identifiers in our server-side counters and logs are one-way SHA-256 hashes from which the original identifier cannot be recovered.
  • Data minimization on the server: by not storing content, we reduce the risk of a server-side leak.

7. Your Rights

You have the following rights with respect to your information:

  • Access: every ticket and every piece of reading material stored on your device can be viewed in the App. Because we hold no copy on our servers, there is no further information for us to disclose.
  • Deletion: use "Delete All" in the App's settings, or uninstall the App. The difference between the two is described in §5.3.
  • Correction: on-device tickets can be corrected using the App's edit features (where available), or by deleting a ticket and placing it again.
  • Data portability (GDPR Art. 20): your data resides on your own device and we hold no copy of it. The version as of 2026-09-22 has no feature for exporting it in a machine-readable format. Because we retain no data server-side, there is no data for us to provide.
  • Restriction and objection: if you do not wish the transmissions described in §1.2 to occur, do not place tickets and do not open tickets to generate reading material. Unless you perform those operations, no ticket text is transmitted to our server.
  • Complaints: contact us using the address in §9 with any complaint regarding this Policy or our handling of your information.
  • Regulatory complaints: EEA / UK users may lodge a complaint with their local data protection authority; users in Japan may contact the Personal Information Protection Commission.

Do-not-sell declaration (CCPA): we do not sell the personal information of any User, including California residents.
